# Whiteout Parking Garage

## Cataclysm: The First Floor Of Winter

The parking garage was cold before anything happened.

Concrete keeps no warmth. Fluorescent tubes buzz over painted numbers, salt
stains, oil rainbows, empty carts, and cars whose owners meant to come back in
an hour. The upper deck looks out over the city, but even that view is framed
by guardrails, ramps, and exhaust-gray pillars.

The Walker's passage does not bring weather from the sky. It gives cold a
place to begin.

Powder lifts from the open edge first, thin enough to dismiss as a gust. Then
the tire tracks fill. A puddle skins over. Headlights turn into pale discs
inside the blowing snow. The ramp down becomes a white tunnel with no bottom,
and every closed car becomes a small sealed room waiting for the temperature to
finish its work.

By the time the Door appears near the exit ramp, the garage is no longer a
building. It is the first shelf of a new ice age.

## Deck-Builder Identity

**Threat verb: freeze.**

Whiteout Parking Garage attacks hand usability. Its hazards do not merely deal
damage or clog the world hand; they lock the player's own cards until the
player finds heat, burns something useful, or waits long enough for the ice to
break.

The central question each turn is:

> Which card do I save, and which card do I burn so the rest of my hand can
> move?

This should feel different from Fog's visibility clock. Fog asks what you can
see. Whiteout asks what you can still use.

## Signature Rule: Frozen Cards And Heat

Some hazards freeze player cards in hand for a small number of turn-start thaw
ticks. A frozen player card remains visible but cannot be played. Heat is a
spendable resource used to thaw cards immediately.

The response kit should let the player:

- gain steady heat;
- thaw one key card cheaply;
- thaw several cards in a burst;
- burn a player card for emergency heat.

The world is fair only if burning a frozen or unwanted card remains a viable
escape valve. A completely locked hand with no burn or passive thaw route is a
failure state, not tension.

## Three-Act Escalation

### Act I: Powder On Concrete

The garage still looks like a place the player can leave.

- Powder drifts and frozen puddles introduce short freezes.
- The Maintenance Closet offers the heat kit before the severe locks arrive.
- The player learns that a card can be visible and still unusable.

### Act II: Ramps Seal Shut

The blizzard moves inside the structure.

- Black ice and snowblind headlights freeze multiple cards or punish partial
  progress.
- The player must decide whether to spend heat on offense now or preserve it
  for a locked future hand.
- Burning cards becomes a real strategy, not only an emergency.

### Act III: The Garage Freezes Shut

The building becomes a freezer with exits painted on the walls.

- Frozen-card count becomes a direct damage risk.
- Slow hazards ask for sustained progress while the player has fewer usable
  cards each turn.
- The Walker arrives as the exit ramp disappears into white air, and the Door
  opens through the ice rather than away from it.

## Hazard Concepts

### Powder Drift

Windblown snow snakes under the guardrail and across the lane markers. It is a
minor ambient card that teaches the mood before the lock pressure gets serious.

### Frozen Puddle

An ordinary oil-slick puddle freezes faster than physics allows. If left
unresolved or partially cleared, it freezes one player card for a short
duration.

### Dead Battery

A car coughs once, then goes silent. Clearing it may grant heat or a basic
warming card; ignoring it leaves the player with one fewer way to restart the
hand.

### Black Ice Ramp

The ramp down shines like glass. It is Slow and punishes delay by freezing
multiple cards, turning escape geometry into a lock.

### Snowblind Headlights

Abandoned headlights flare through the whiteout. They make the hand feel
wrongly bright: everything visible, nothing warm enough to trust.

### Ice-Locked Door

A stairwell fire door is sealed shut under hoarfrost. Partial progress cracks
it but sprays freezing air back into the hand.

### Plow Buried In Snow

A city plow sits abandoned halfway through the upper level, already half a
snowbank. It is a heavy obstacle that can become a major heat or progress
opportunity if cleared.

### The Garage Freezes Shut

The signature threat. The concrete structure becomes one piece of ice. At end
of turn, it freezes cards and punishes every frozen card the player is carrying.

## Reward Card Concepts

### Hand Warmers

Gain a small amount of Heat. Repeatable, plain, and reliable.

### Ice Scraper

Thaw one card and make modest progress. It is the workhorse that turns heat
back into action.

### Burn The Manual

Destroy one player card in hand to gain a larger burst of Heat. The world asks
the player to treat plans as fuel.

### Space Heater

Exhaust for a large Heat gain. Strong when drawn early, precious when the hand
is almost locked.

### Jumper Cables

Thaw two cards in a burst, then exhaust. It converts saved heat into one
decisive turn.

## Walker Interaction

The Walker does not summon a storm like an enemy spell. It proves that cold can
choose a starting point.

Every footprint is warmer than the world around it for one second, then colder
than everything else forever. The player follows those footprints through
parking rows, around dead cars, under frozen fluorescent fixtures, until the
pattern leads to the exit ramp. The Door waits there, rimed in violet light,
opening into somewhere that has not yet frozen.

## Visual Intrusion

The reality backdrop should remain the mundane upper garage: concrete columns,
low ceiling, parked cars, salt stains, amber fixtures, and one trace of powder
at the exterior edge.

The intrusion overlay should show the First Floor Of Winter around the
perimeter:

- whiteout gusts pouring in from the open deck edge and ramp mouths;
- blue-white ice crawling across columns, ceiling seams, and tire tracks;
- frozen cars half-buried at the edges, not centered under the play area;
- fluorescent amber light turning brittle inside blown snow;
- repeated footprint geometry freezing darker than the surrounding concrete;
- frost fractures bending toward the future Door location.

Keep the center readable for cards and HUD. The catastrophe should feel like a
parking garage being converted into the first storage level of winter.
