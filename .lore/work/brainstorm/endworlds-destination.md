# Three End Worlds — Destination or Denial?

## Narrative Anchor

The Walker is running from Death. Someone they love is dying, and if the Destiny chases the Walker it can't chase their love. The entire game is flight disguised as survival. Apocalypse is not something wrong with reality — it is the only thing making "here and now" unbearable enough to abandon someone you care for.

Every world has an underlying music theme of hopelessness. The player follows because every broken world feels preferable to standing still long enough to watch someone slip away. The music was never about the Walker running — it was about everything he was running from.

## World 13: The Quiet Before Noise

**Intro to the real story.** The first world where nothing attacks initially. The silence is worse than any apocalypse because there's no collapse to blame for once. The music shifts — still melancholy but something underneath changes. You hear why what they built together mattered, or you hear their last conversation played back without words.

**Mechanics:**
- World cards appear balanced between constructive and destructive effects with neither dominating until the player *makes a choice*.
- Aggressive play (treating every card as something to overcome): cards gain Refusal keyword — the insistence that more trying is enough.
- Defensive or discard-forward play: cards gain Acknowledgment keyword — slows down but builds toward clarity.
- By Act 3, keyword dominance shifts what happens on clearance, opening the player onto different emotional trajectories through identical mechanical encounters.

## World 14: The Bargain

**Where you name what you'd sacrifice.** Opens normal for three turns then the Walker's presence cracks the world completely. Every Door he opened across all previous worlds appears visible in distance — eleven violet fractures hanging suspended like broken windows — and the music builds into something that sounds like every one of those twelve worlds crying out at once.

**Mechanics:**
- Cards are brutally efficient but every play costs Memory Fragments or Feats — permanent progression earned across dozens of runs disappears when these cards hit.
- The player trades accumulated Destiny to fight this battle, literally trading what they've built world by world.
- By the time you open for World 15 you have nothing left but whatever core strategy survived all that spending. Either fully committed or forced into stillness through your own choices.

## World 15: One Door Two Paths

**The split happens here.** Mid-run, based on Refusal vs Acknowledgment accumulated across worlds 13 and 14. No boss fight — this is not a game about combat at the end; it's about why running felt like an option in the first place.

**If Refusal dominates:**
- The Door opens to a war zone where Death itself has form because refusal gave it shape.
- Fight not with deck but with whatever Feats and Unlocks survived the bargain — every move costs something irreplaceable.
- "Winning" ending shows Death stepping back only to watch you carry everything forever while becoming exactly what you ran from: a force moving through worlds breaking them all in pursuit of something impossible.

**If Acknowledgment dominates:**
- The Door opens not to another world but to hers — final reality rendered without intrusion palette for the first time.
- No apocalypse bleeding outward. Just her room, her face, that last hour stretched across turns you cannot speed.
- Cards become gestures rather than strategies — each one an action she would understand. You play through saying what couldn't be said running out of time but choosing to stay present until the music resolves not with catastrophe but completion.

**Both endings are marked equal because both are honestly human.** The Walker stops walking in either path. The game ends. Neither is marked better.

## New Stat: Attachment (meta-progression)

Invisible stat tracking throughout all fifteen worlds:
- Climbs when you play aggressively, take risks, keep cards instead of discarding them.
- Drops when you slow down and prioritize discards over plays and make conservative choices.

**Revealed retroactively in World 13:** what each "lucky" run meant — every desperate survival? Attachment was climbing behind the scenes. Every careful strategy? You were already practicing acceptance without knowing why. The game rewinds itself to show what emotional state created each path taken, giving context to everything before it.

## Design Tension

The whole point: does accepting Death honor the Walker's purpose (protecting his love from further harm in another moment) or negate the entire run he just completed? Both readings are defensible because both come from real human grief responses — fight vs surrender with neither more right than the other, only different.
